Who? Roseanne Cherrie Barr is an American actress, comedian, writer, and television producer. Barr began her career in stand-up comedy at clubs before gaining critical and popular acclaim in the television sitcom Roseanne. ~ Wikipedia

Podcast Narration

Roseanne Barr has shown us just how far the line in America’s sand has drifted from the realm of basic decency.

She got the post-Memorial-Day week off to a racist start with a tweet describing Valerie Jarrett, a former adviser to President Barack Obama, as someone who looks like the “Muslim brotherhood & planet of the apes had a baby.”

Barr quickly defended her Tuesday morning tweet as a “joke.” She then tweeted that she was leaving Twitter. Five minutes later she returned to Twitter with a more formal and entirely unbelievable apology.

Hours after ABC canceled her reboot of “Roseanne” because of her racist tweet about a former senior adviser to President Barack Obama, Ms. Barr returned to the platform and posted more than 100 times. She tweeted, retweeted, reply-tweeted and, in many cases, deleted tweets from her account, @therealroseanne, which has been active since March 2011 and has 774,000 followers.

My thoughts are Barr’s tweet about Jarrett is highly offensive. I don’t blame ABC for almost immediately canceling her show, which preempted an exodus of advertisers.

Below you will find a large number of comments made about the situation on social media.

First, I do NOT approve of her tweet. It was disgusting.

However, I feel canceling her show was NOT the correct response. It seems disproportionate and a gendered reaction to a joke in poor taste.

I recently watched all episodes of the new Roseanne show. Topics ranged from the challenges of being poor and having medical needs to the challenges of being a single parent to the challenges of taking a sucky job to take care of your family to the challenges of alcohol addiction. And yes, there was even an episode that centered on the discomfort of having a Muslim neighbor move in next door an learning to respect them.

This was an incredibly well-written show that tackled MANY of the issues nobody else on main-stream TV is touching in a way that could reach people across political lines.

Canceling this show was a huge mistake and it feels like a gendered punishment. For men to have their shows canceled (Charlie Rose, i.e.) they have had to be sexual predators. Roseanne made a joke in poor taste, deleted it immediately, learned from her mistake, and apologized. And her show was canceled, probably 100 people are now out of work. Deep in my heart, this is not OK. If a man (Trump) had made the same comment there would not have been such a severe response.

I agree I don’t watch for the actors but the show. The personal beliefs of the actor are completely unimportant and Not relevant to me

I don’t want to hear any actors, or athletics, or sports persons political views, then when they have it sewn into what they do, I am out

Roseanne has been “edgy” her entire career. She has a history of saying provocative things. What did they expect??? I think she was targeted by an industry that leans a different way politically. My biggest issue though is why are her (or any actor for that matter…) comments so important to deserve such a response? She is an actress, not an elected public official, not hired for her political/racial/social view, etc. She is hired to read a script. (or write/direct one). Her “views” are irrelevant to me as long as she acts while on camera. Why do we give actors such power in areas they have no ability in? I don’t ask my uber driver for medical advise. I don’t care what my mechanics political affiliation is, as long as he fixes my car. I really don’t care what Roseanne (or Ashley Judd, Madonna, Whoopie, etc) believe or say. Not relavant…

It wasn’t just a “view”. It was a nasty racist remark about. a person she doesn’t even know. and then when she tried to “cover it up” she made it worse with her next 2 or 3 racist remarks.

NO — Roseanne had her chance and she got what she deserved. Nobody did that to her. She did it to herself.

you miss my point…. Roseanne’s firing is a contractual matter between her and her employer (whichever network it was). Maybe it was deserved, maybe not. I havent bothered to research what she actually said (vs what she is accused of MEANING in what she said), or whether it was hateful or racist. She is just an actor and it doesnt deserve the major importance it seems to be given. Her comments are “significant” because society makes her a VIP. If she was “Joe Anybody” nobody would raise a stink and she wouldnt have lost her job (like my mechanic or Uber driver example). Actors have a long history of saying really stupid stuff — this is just one more example. Why do we choose to make them such significant people who’s opinion is so much more important than normal people? I DO think it is sad that “they” can say anything they want as long as it isnt racist or sexist (at this intersection of our society) and its perfectly ok (look at all the nasty things being said in the political realm). We have a dog whistle mentality at the present for those two areas, but ignore the rest. I’m not trying to justify racism or sexism, but I think we have become too sentitive to words…

I can quite easily imagine a male actor having a show canceled for comparing black people to monkeys, a comparison used to dehumanize them for some 500 years.

I had similar thoughts. I just wish there might be a consequence that didn’t harm so many people.

This was my reaction, too. Both the old and new iterations of the show had characters on all sides of the political/cultural spectrum, and the narrow-minded characters were routinely corrected by the situations created by the writers, akin to All In The Family (Archie Bunker) or South Park. Eric Cartman is my favorite sitcom character, because you can always expect him to act exclusively in his own interest in ways that inevitably lead to his downfall.

Some tinfoil: I have a suspicion that all of this is a ruse. The show was cancelled not for the Planet of the Apes comment, but because Roseanne criticized George Soros.

They cancelled Garrison Keillor.

You’re absolutely right that things will be hard for the people who lose their jobs because of the cancellation, and that’s a tragedy. And the show did seem to be connecting with viewers across our new cultural divide.

On balance, I do respectfully disagree though. With Tom, I think a pattern of racism (it wasn’t just one joke — she’s done comparable racist tweets for years, I learned recently) is serious enough to warrant firing. And without Roseanne, there isn’t “Roseanne,” so the show’s cancellation was pretty inevitable once ABC decided they couldn’t have her working with them.

And ABC wasn’t the only party who decided they couldn’t work with her. Wanda Sykes had already quit writing for the show even more quickly than ABC canceled it. I doubt that the mostly liberal writers and actors who had other opportunities would all be eager to continue working with her. An exodus of writers and even actors seems to have been inevitable. ABC was just getting out ahead of it.

Also, one more thing: while Trump didn’t have his show canceled, he was fired from “The Celebrity Apprentice” for his racist about Mexican immigrants:

​Donald Trump officially fired from “The Celebrity Apprentice”

Of course, Trump became president afterward. Maybe Roseanne will too. (Ugh!)

Whatever your thoughts on the subject are at least we are seeing that there are consequences.
