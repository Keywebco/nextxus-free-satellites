narration

Every day is earth day! Or should be! Earth Day is an environmental movement which began in 1970. It is a call to action for the global community to talk about climate change, adopt a sustainable, healthy environment and protect our planet for future generations.

This year let’s ‘End Plastic Pollution’. What can you do to help?

Earth-friendly things you and your kids can do to celebrate 🌏 Earth Day every day.

  1. Get Friends to hand out bags and clean up a nearby park.
  2. Plant shrubs or a tree In your yard
  3. Start a vegetable garden In pots ora small plot,
  4. Organize a cleanup day at your School or Office.
  5. Create posters With environmental themes and ask teachers to post them In their classrooms or business in windows.
  6. Look for recycling symbols on products you or your parents buy. Purchase Items In recyclable packaging whenever possible, Avoid products that use excessive packaging.
  7. Be sure to carry a reusable shopping bag, and buy them In bulk to save money and reduce plastic and container waste.
  8. Bicycle or walk to school or work rather-than being driven by your parents (as long as Mom and Dad say It’s safe). As an adult cut out those short drives you don’t really need to drive to.
  9. Steer toward organic pesticides, such as those made from orange extract.
  10. Turn off lights, Fans or the TV when you leave the room.
  11. Check your home for leaky faucets or toilets, and volunteer to help those who can fix these issues.
  12. Don’t leave the water running while brushing your teeth or washing your face and hands. Take shorter showers.
  13. Use a broom Instead of a hose to clean the driveway or sidewalk. Reminder that washing the car less often saves water (and may get you out of the dreaded chore).
  14. Heating and Cooling raise (In the summer) or lower (In the winter) the thermostat a few degrees. This will save energy as well as money.
  15. Lower The blinds when the sun comes up. This deflects heat from the wIndows.
  16. Often replace air ﬁlters. A clean filter helps the air-conditioner run more efficiently

Earth Day 2018 | Earth Day Network

Corporate social responsibility (CSR) has gained more interest in the past decade, however it is not a new idea; it dates back to the 1930s, said Eric Orts of the University of Pennsylvania. Just before World War II, German industrialist Walter Rathenau claimed that business corporations had become very large and that they had grown to be a significant part of the society. According to Rathenau, even though fundamentally a corporation’s intent is the pursuit of private interests and profits for owners of the company, they are increasingly bearing the marks of an undertaking and, to an increasing degree, have been serving the public interest (Kessler, 1930). Further, philosophers John Dewey and James H. Tufts, in their book Ethics (1908), raised the concept that it is not sufficient to view companies as purely economic machines and that companies should be involved in public duty as well.
