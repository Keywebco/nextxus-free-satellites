Podcast Narration by eCom Tips

Does it really do you any good? Have you convinced anyone of anything? What could you have done to better your life during the 10 minutes you spent typing out an argumentative response on Facebook?

We either love or hate Facebook, or we just pay attention every now and then when we get bored and want to mindlessly scroll around to see what our friends and family have been up to.

I have no research to support this claim but I’ll just say it: I would dare to guess that those that love Facebook see it as a tool to promote themselves, or their businesses. They enjoy the possibility of gaining exposure to the almost 2.19 billion users (as of 2018 cited by this source).

I have several music projects that are actively performing every weekend, so I hope that music lovers and concert goers see my virtual event flyers like this one and they find time in their schedule to come check out the shows. And then personally I have many close friends and family on FB that enjoy seeing pics of my little one running around, growing up, and discovering everything for the first time. So my personal and professional needs all end up being supported across one platform.

I would guess that if you hate Facebook, you at one time enjoyed it, but your feed is poorly managed, and you are probably bombarded with tons of political opinion, product marketing, and/or “friends” who see the world in such a different view that it’s hard to wrap your head around. So you start to build a certain intolerance for things that annoy you about the platform and it seems that every time that you allow yourself to try and get back in, you keep hitting a wall of negative posts, political “fake news”, and time wasting video meme trash of the internet.

I get it.. and there are plenty of Medium posts that I’ve read lately that suggest users of all age and form are starting to understand that they’ve gotten too far down a terrible hole of FB crap, and instead of creating a strategy to clean up their feed, customizing advertising prefs, and unfollowing toxic “friends” or business Pages, they just say..

“Screw this… DELETE!”

I have chosen to keep Facebook in my life.

Somewhere deep down, I want to take the time to make it a positive experience. I want be inspired by friends, family, and businesses that I follow, and I want to deliver that same experience to people that follow me.

So I wade through the trash, ignore the political BS, unfollow things that waste my time, and I try to keep a very positive sharing experience, and I promote this on both my personal and business page in order to practice what I preach.

Still, there are those posts that you read, or videos that you watch that get you so fired up! It’s so hard to not get wrapped up in the emotional entanglement, where the internet traps you for maybe 10 minutes or so. You feel like “I just have to say something”.. “These people don’t understand what they are talking about”.. or.. “How can anyone feel that way.. I’m going to give them a piece of my mind”…

Okay.. probably nobody says that last statement out loud.. HA. But we feel compelled for whatever reason to argue our point.. and at the end of day.. no one is better off. No one reads your opinion, and then thinks, “Wow, I feel better.”

I’m sorry, it just doesn’t work like that.

So in lieu of wasting 10 minutes using your “fightin’ words” trying so desperately to make people understand that you are right and they are wrong:

Here are 10 better uses of your time, and hopefully 10 ways to feel better, stay positive, and live a higher quality of life alongside all this digital mayhem.

1) Pour yourself a cold glass of water

Water is essential to our body. Up to 60% of our body is made of water. When you are dehydrated you can get headaches, dry skin, become dizzy, and feel tired and lazy. Take a break, put down the device, drink a glass of water.. feel better?

2) Inhale.. count to 5.. exhale..repeat

Take a few minutes and breathe. Focus on your breathing and calm your body. Doing this in a quiet place, away from distraction will help. Put the technology down, stand or sit upright in place, close your eyes, and take a couple of deep breaths. You will lower your heart rate. You will feel the tension release a bit around your shoulders and neck.

3) Walk outside, look up, look around, what do you see?

It’s amazing to me how many times we walk past or under something in our daily lives, not paying attention… But if we just take a second to look up, observe the space around us, we might find something fresh, or have a realization that we’ve never had before.

Sometimes you’ll see things for the first time, objectively, surprisingly. Maybe you notice the stars in the night sky. Maybe you notice a tree, or a structure, or architecture you’ve never seen before. My clichéd commentary here is that you will open yourself up to possibility, to reflection, to nature, and maybe you’ll take a moment to think about how small and somewhat insignificant you are, or how unimportant the tension is that you feel.

4) Locate a pet, sit down, and rub their head and around the back of their ears.

I know that’s random.. but pets do like it when you rub around their ears and work on their head. Pets give us unconditional love. They are forever appreciative of your warmth and support. They want that forever home, and that comfort. And when you give them attention, they are so quick to respond and love you right back. Suddenly FB seems so small and so unimportant. The present moment, the love that is transferred.. it comes right back.

5) Take a moment to say “I Love You” to your loved ones.

Here you are on Facebook all riled up, about to lay down a stream of digits that enforce corrective thinking on the populous… Is it ever really gonna matter as much as when you stop, look around, find a loved one, and bundle all of that crazy energy to let them know that you appreciate them in your life? It’s easier said than done right? Is it possible to collect all of the negative energy, but then still output a positive message? It’s almost impossible for some.. but it’s worth a try.

6) Go for a walk / Get some physical exercise

Physical exercise gets the blood pumping, and raises our energy levels. Here you are feeling very negative about a FB post, so a swift pop of posivitity to break you away from it might help. Put the phone down, get outside, or get the gym and see what happens.

From webmd.com:

When you exercise, your body releases chemicals called endorphins. These endorphins interact with the receptors in your brain that reduce your perception of pain. Endorphins also trigger a positive feeling in the body.

7) Complete some yard work / Work in your garden / Plant a flower

To go along with #6, find a physical outlet to harness your negative energy for good. I actually hate yardwork, truth be told. However, the strangest thing happens while I’m cutting the grass. I have about a hour while I’m winding the mower back and forth across the lawn where I’m deep in thought. The roar of the mower falls into the background of my conscience and I think about life, my goals, and how I’m spending my time.

Some people enjoy gardening for some of the same reasons. You can easily find a zen in moments. It breaks you from the tension of the interwebs. It’s nature’s way of pulling you back into focus. It’s beautiful and although I say I hate cutting the grass.. it’s really the “responsibility” of dealing with a yard. The physical act is somewhat therapeutic.

8) Take a shower, shave, and get cleaned up

I mean.. who in the world feels terrible after a shower. Self-explanitory I think. I don’t shave because I have a long beard that I keep up. However, I do tend to the beard after the shower.. HA. After everything is clean, neat, and fresh, you just feel 1000% better about your day.

9) Prepare a meal

I’m not a cook, but occasionally my wife and I put together a tasty meal. I do get up in the mornings and make breakfast for my family. Those minutes in front of the stove, minding the pots, setting the heat just right, adding the right ingredients.. this is its own form of zen in the kitchen.

Occasionally I make the mistake of hoping on Facebook when I get out of bed in the morning. I’M SORRY.. It’s a terrible habit.. one that I definitely am working to break. It royally messes up my thought in the morning. I start thinking about all the drama in my feed posted by friends and family, and I haven’t even told my wife that I love her yet. No bueno..

But when we get downstairs, and I start cooking for the morning breakfast. I feel better, and it helps me re-center my mood and attitude for the day.

10) Take a moment to be thankful.. Express gratitude

This could come in so many forms. I’ve written previously about expressing gratitude in journal form every morning. I slip from time to time and I notice that on days that I skip the gratitude journal and instead choose to peruse Facebook or other social media, I leave the bedroom to start my day with a nervous tension. When I express gratitude, in lieu of scrolling Facebook, I leave the bedroom with head held high. My mind is refreshed. I’ve honored those who I look up to, and been thankful for those that inspire me, and this mindfulness alone allows for a huge boost of positivity.

The next time you are upset, and you feel the need to fight on a post, or make someone aware of your specific view, back up a second, put the device aside a second. Think of something or someone in your life that you trust, that you love, that you are thankful for. Once again, this is a tiny action that calms your mind, gives you pause, and possibly gives you enough time to compose yourself.

Even on the other end of the internet, you can feel the tension through a person’s words. But you can also feel when someone is honestly contributing, open, and genuine in discussion. Being grateful for the ability to connect with people all over the world should allow you to bring your attitude into check, and allow you to communicate with respect. Your perspective is important, but if you value life, you’ll value your friends and family’s perspective as well.

I had so many ideas when putting this post together. Should I make it satirical? Should I preach? Should I ask?

I just wanted to express a theme of pause. We are so lucky to live in a day and age when we can connect with the world just with a couple of clicks. But then we become consumed so fast. We rush to judgement, and we lash out at each other.. For what?

What is it really worth when you spend 10 minutes calling others out, arguing with people that don’t understand your point, and worse yet, calling people names and giving them labels? It’s just not worth the time and effort and if you spent that time working on bettering yourself, and loving the ones around you, my thought is that you’ll have a much more fulfilled life.

Thoughts?
