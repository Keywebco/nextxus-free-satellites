Podcast Narration

Why I Don’t Give Money To Street People. I live in Las Vegas and there seems to be people begging for money on every street corner. I do not give them money. Want to know why?

First of all I am not a heartless person. I do care about the street people and wish to help them. But I will not give them money.

Here are the reasons why:

They will spend it on drugs or alcohol.

They sleep on the streets and are subject to crime from drive by shooter and others. When they sleep on the street they create a nuisance to land owners. Homeless Shelters are the first and most obvious choice when it comes down to where they can stay the night. They have their pros and cons, which is why you still see lots of people camping out on the streets instead of staying inside their local shelters. Most places are only free for a limited amount of time, and then they charge a fee. It could be a week or a month later when your “free time” runs out. So not everybody can afford to stay in them, while others live like nomads and travel from shelter to shelter, staying only until they use up all their free time. Bed bugs can also be a very common problem at certain shelters. Homeless people don’t always have the best hygiene and will often stay in motel rooms or other places on and off and then in shelters, which can help spread bed bugs. Another thing to keep in mind is that many organizations require that you attend certain services or participate in some type of program in order to stay with them. So while shelters can definitely be a safe option, it’s not always the cheapest, the most hygienic, or the best option for those who want to feel free.

Eventually they find more secluded areas, like under bridges and overpasses, but anybody can attacked them in their sleep in these spots. So they eventually will a parking lot outside a closed business to set up a tent at night. They will break it down every morning and the business owners didn’t seem to mind as long as they left before they arrived each morning.

In Las Vegas, many homeless people stay in an underground sewer system that has many dry areas, which runs beneath the entire city. Which is a high risk because we often have flash floods. Many have been caught and killed in the flash floods.

Some will set up camp in abandoned houses. Creating health and fire hazard. Not counting being a nuisance to the nearby homes. The home owners will call the police, then the police will run them off, only for them to return days later.

A lot of street people are mentally ill and need help. They have alienated themselves from family and friends.

The top causes of being homeless among families were: (1) lack of affordable housing, (2) unemployment, (3) poverty, and (4) low wages, in that order.

How To Help The Homeless:

Show some respect. Don’t treat a homeless person as if she were invisible. Say, “good morning,” when you pass or strike up a conversation on a park bench. Many people experiencing homelessness say that the loss of dignity that accompanies their situation is harder to bear than the actual loss of physical things.

Donate. Clothing is a big one here, as are shoes and food. Non-perishable items are always in short supply at food pantries and homeless shelters. Other items that might be needed include blankets, coats, books and small kitchen items, such as cups and utensils. If you are donating to a homeless shelter or another organization that helps the homeless, consider donating office supplies, electronics, appliances, phone cards or other items that might help those who help the homeless. If you see someone who is homeless in winter, offer blankets, food or tarps (if you can’t convince them to go to a shelter).

Reach out to them. Invite a homeless person to your place of worship or a local community event. Organize an outing to take homeless kids to the movies or an ice skating rink. Offer moral support when you can.

Be an advocate for them. Call your local homeless shelters to find out what items are needed, then contact your local scout troops or civic organizations to organize food drives or other fundraising events to pull those items together. Follow local politics and speak up at town council meetings on issues of homelessness and programs for the homeless. Write editorial letters to your local newspaper about the issue of homelessness in your community and what people can do to help.

Many shelters also provide services such as alcohol and drug rehab treatment along with clinics. How to find a homeless shelter in your area: Homeless Shelters

No, I won’t give them money, but I will donate to homeless shelters and furnish them with a coat, gloves or blanket. I will buy food at the local fast food business and give them a hamburger, etc.

Linda Kelly Lee
