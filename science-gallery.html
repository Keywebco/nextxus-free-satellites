Science Gallery — NASA ViewSpace and the Smithsonian sky

Free interactive tours of the universe, from NASA — the model Roger named for the art gallery, used here for science itself. ViewSpace is a free, web-based collection of digital interactives and videos on astronomy and Earth science, provided free with the support of NASA and produced by the Space Telescope Science Institute (verified live 2026-11-19).

NASA ViewSpace — interactive image tours Truth Gate standard: 95% Open directly

The three ViewSpace Image Tours — verified live 2026-11-19

  • Center of the Milky Way Galaxy — eleven Tour Stops through Hubble, Spitzer, and Chandra X-ray telescope images: the Brick, a dense dark cloud of hydrogen molecules imaged by Spitzer, and a zoom toward the supermassive black hole, Sagittarius A*, at the galaxy’s core.
  • Herbig-Haro 46/47 — a pair of young, forming stars shaping their nebula through ejections of gas and dust, in a James Webb Space Telescope image. Webb turns up a distant galaxy in the background famous for its resemblance to a question mark.
  • The Whirlpool Galaxy — the iconic spiral galaxy’s swirling arms, glowing core, and surrounding stars, with insights into star formation and galaxy structure, in a Hubble Space Telescope image.

Google’s Street View went underwater with the Catlin Seaview Survey: six coral reefs you can swim through from your screen, including the Great Barrier Reef, Molokini Crater in Hawaii, and Apo Island in the Philippines (verified live 2026-11-19). Flight simulator — God’s-eye view from the cockpit

GeoFS is a free flight simulator that runs in a web browser with nothing to install: global satellite images, 30+ aircraft, multiplayer, real-life air traffic, and real weather (verified live 2026-11-19). The free tier flies on standard-resolution imagery; HD imagery is a paid subscription, said plainly — the free tier is the whole simulator.

Learn by being there — documents and immersive visuals

If we are going to have educators, they might as well really be able to educate. The method is simple: pick a free video, do a voice-over with it, and let other people’s work fill the gaps. Nothing here is rebuilt that already exists for free.

The document system — How It’s Made, and systems like it

The official How It’s Made channel on YouTube (run by Cineflix, verified live 2026-11-19) carries hundreds of free segments — how things are made, five minutes at a time. Full episodes are not free there; they stream free with ads on Tubi and Plex, and by subscription on Max and Discovery+ (see the where-to-watch listing). The document system works the same way for any subject: link the free material, and voice-over it.

Float inside a nuclear reactor

When a professor tutors about a nuclear reaction, the learner should be able to float around inside a reactor — or a graphic of one:

  • Temelín Nuclear Power Plant virtual tour — a free virtual walk through a real working plant in Czechia: the control room, the reactor hall, the containment building, and the cooling towers (VVER-1000 reactors, verified live 2026-11-19).
  • EBR-I at Idaho National Laboratory — free virtual tours of the world’s first nuclear power plant, the reactor that first made usable electricity in 1951; live guided tours by email, and 360-degree photos to explore alone (verified live 2026-11-19).
  • Chernobyl Atlas — a free 3D model of the RBMK reactor’s power block in your browser: drag to turn it, open it in five stages into 26 systems and about 7,200 pieces (verified live 2026-11-19). A stylised model, not a survey of the real plant, and it does not show the 1986 accident.
