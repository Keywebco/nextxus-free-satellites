Every Adult Should Be Able To Tie A Tie.

Before you start

· Stand in front of a mirror and flip your collar up.
· Tie with the seam side down.
· Put the wide end on your right, narrow end on your left.
· The wide end should hang about 12 in (30 cm) lower than the narrow end. Adjust for your height.

1. Four-in-Hand — easiest, most common

Best for: almost any collar, casual to business.

1. Cross the wide end over the narrow end.
2. Bring the wide end under the narrow end and up through the neck loop.
3. Bring the wide end across the front of the narrow end.
4. Bring the wide end down through the front loop that forms.
5. Tighten by pulling the wide end down and sliding the knot up to your collar.
6. Make a small dimple just below the knot if you want a polished look.

2. Half-Windsor — medium, symmetrical

Best for: medium-spread collars, business settings.

· Start like the Four-in-Hand.
· After bringing the wide end up through the neck loop, wrap it around the narrow end one extra time.
· Then bring it across the front and down through the front loop.
· Tighten. It gives a neat, slightly larger knot than the Four-in-Hand.

3. Full Windsor — large, formal

Best for: spread or cutaway collars, formal events.

· Start like the Half-Windsor, but make two full passes around the narrow end before finishing.
· Bring the wide end up through the neck loop, around, under, and up again.
· Finish by bringing it down through the front loop and tightening.
· Needs a longer tie. Creates a big, triangular knot.

4. Pratt / Shelby — medium, neat

Best for: everyday business wear.

· Start with the tie seam side out.
· Cross the narrow end over the wide end.
· Bring the narrow end up through the neck loop, across the front, and down through the front loop.
· Tighten, then flip the tie down so the seam is hidden.

Other methods

· Kelvin — easy, slightly larger than Four-in-Hand.
· Oriental / Simple — small, neat knot.
· Nicky — medium knot, easy to dimple.
· Balthus — large, ceremonial, many wraps.
· Eldredge — decorative, braided look, advanced.
· Merovingian — multi-wrap, decorative.
· Trinity — Celtic-style, decorative.

Quick tips

· Match the knot to the collar: narrow collar → small knot; spread collar → larger knot.
· The tie tip should ideally reach your belt buckle.
· Tighten slowly and shape the knot with your fingers.
· Left-handed? Just mirror the instructions.

If you’re learning, master the Four-in-Hand first, then the Half-Windsor, then the Full Windsor.
