Your Child Has Autism — What Helps Right Now

You are already doing the most important thing

You are paying attention. You love your child. That matters more than any perfect plan. A diagnosis does not change who your child is. It gives you better language and tools to support them.

It is normal to feel shocked, sad, overwhelmed, or even relieved. All of those can be true at the same time. You do not have to have everything figured out today.

What autism is (simple and accurate)

Autism is a neurodevelopmental difference. It affects how a person processes sensory information, communicates, and handles change. It is a spectrum — every child is different. There is no single cause and no single “look.”

Many autistic children also have strengths: deep focus, honesty, pattern recognition, memory, and intense interests that can become gifts.

First practical steps

  • Talk with your child’s doctor about early intervention or therapy referrals (speech, occupational therapy, behavioral support).
  • Ask about developmental services in your state or country. Early support often helps.
  • Start noticing sensory patterns (noise, light, clothing, food textures, transitions).
  • Use simple visual schedules and clear warnings before changes.
  • Protect rest time for yourself. You cannot pour from an empty cup.

Meltdowns vs. tantrums

Meltdowns are usually overload, not willful defiance. The child is overwhelmed and cannot process more input. Safety first, reduce sensory load, stay calm, say very little, and wait. Punishment usually makes it worse. Recovery takes time.
