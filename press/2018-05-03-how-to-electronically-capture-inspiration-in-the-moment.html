Tools and hacks to capture ideas right when they surface so you can use them for your next story, social post, or lyric writing.

Audio Narration

I was having a random conversation with a friend about a year ago. We were discussing life experiences that we had before we crossed paths.

Sometimes it’s interesting to sit down and talk to someone that you think you know well, but as they uncover a past experience in life you start to realize that there’s so much more to their story.

My friend honorably served time in the military. Although I was previously aware of this, our conversation uncovered a few details about his deployments that blew my mind.

He described a horrific training exercise in which the men in his regiment were purposefully tormented, and specifically pushed to their physical and mental breaking points. He explained that the reason this exercise was so intense was because the area in which he was being deployed was susceptible to enemy capture. And if you were captured, the military’s code of conduct read that you would endure everything in your power to not break and/or release any information. 😯

Anyway, so he’s telling me this, and describing the training in detail, and it hits me.. This would be incredible material to use for lyrics in a song!

I write heavy metal music for one of my original projects where the lyrics potentially cover dark emotional content. But instead of focusing on the negative, most of my lines describe the negativity, but the subject ends up finding a way to overcome the hurdles and rise above the conflict. So, generally, I twist the conflict into a positive triumphant resolution.

Note: this conversation with my friend was a very specific moment for me that I wanted to use in this post to illustrate a point where inspiration hit me hard and I had to act to capture it.

I felt the need to immediately capture my ideas based on the concepts and specific emotions that I was feeling while taking in this crazy information!

The whole time we were talking my mind was racing! All I could think about was ‘this could be used to create a monstrous emotional song regarding the power of the mind; the darkest places, the frustrations, the torment, but then finding the light, discovering a break-through, and the triumph of the human spirit to endure the pain in order to win in the end!

I literally couldn’t get my phone out fast enough. I use electronic means among everything else these days to capture my ideas. And although I don’t have anything against running and grabbing a pen and paper (because if that’s what you need to do, then do it!), sometimes the smart phone is the absolute most perfect tool to pull out, tap on an app that captures voice or text, and record away!

You never know when inspiration is going to hit.

Here are a few actions that I take to immediately capture ideas electronically when the inspiration hits:

1) Ask Siri to take a note

Siri is the personal assistant that most are familiar with on the Apple platform. On the iPhone, you can simply say “Hey Siri” or depress the iPhone’s Home button (iPhone 8) or side button (iPhone X) and say “Take a note”. Siri then asks what you want the note to say, and you can just start speaking your ideas directly. “Okay Google” will work on Android, and almost every other platform has some form of personal assistant that will capture your voice ideas.

If it’s more of a long form note where you have a lot to say, what I would suggest is create the note and state the main thought, allow Siri to confirm that the note is created, and then tap the note on the confirm screen immediately jumping you directly into that specific note. From there you can continue to dictate your voice, or you can just start typing out supporting text to work through the ideas.

If you are an Evernote user, you can also ask Siri to create a note in your Evernote app. Siri will create the note and capture your voice. The note will be placed in the default folder that you select when organizing your Evernote account.

2) Capture a reminder

Another thing you can ask your personal assistant to do is set a reminder. Or at the least you can launch a reminder app on your phone write the main idea and set a follow up. Some programs add reminders to calendar apps and if that works for you then do it. I use the Reminders App on my phone to capture all sorts of information.

Specifically for the purposes of this post, I use Reminders to remind me to come back to or “remember” a thought process that I was having. In other words I can say something to Siri like, “Remind me to finish out my thought process regarding X, Y, Z..”. In essence I’m capturing the main thought, and then reminding myself to take a few minutes to sit down later and flush out the ideas around the main thought.

In addition to this, I have my OmniFocus app programmed to look at my Reminders App. If a new reminder hits the list, OmniFocus grabs the reminder and loads it into my OmniFocus inbox. This process is automated. What happens next is that I will eventually open OmniFocus to clear out the inbox and see what my next task should be, and I see the reminder sitting there. From this point I can add more information to the note section, and I can give it a defer date for popping up later in my workflow, or I can add a due date, if it’s an item that’s mission critical that needs to be handled on a certain date and time.

3) Take a picture

Is a picture worth a thousand words? It could mean everything when you come across something fast and you need a quick capture. It could be a billboard, or a poster, or an item that you walk by everyday that you just happen to get a different perspective of. Maybe jotting down notes or voice dictation just isn’t right in the moment so a snap shot is the perfect action to take.

These days digital storage for photos and videos is electronically busting at the seams because of how easy it is (and actually super efficient and high quality the media can be) from our smart phones. If capturing a photo is the key to allow you to remember an important piece of inspiring content, then by all means do it, just have a way to easily recall that photo at a later date so that it doesn’t get lost in the shuffle with all the other digital media clogging up your harddrives.

On the iPhone, when I review a picture that I just took, I can immediately “heart” it, essentially adding it to a favorites folder. This at least helps seperate a collection of photos over and above the sea of everything else, so that when I review my photos, my favorite content is conveniently bundled together for easy access.

4) Take a screenshot

Very similar to number 3, but helpful nonetheless. Figure out your phone’s ability to screenshot the digital page that you are scrolling. Screen shots normally are saved into your photo storage, and on the iPhone, screenshots are automatically bundled together without any extra action from you. This is great so that you can always refer back to your screenshot album to recall the information that you wanted to save.

To take this a step further, the iPhone now allows you to add writing, drawing, magnifiers, and more to your screenshots, before you either send them somewhere or save them to your photo storage.

You can also circle or lasso certain important text or items pictured. You can use arrows to point out important parts of your screenshots (these tools are also available for your regular digital photos of course).

Last but not least, use the highlighter function to highlight text passages. Maybe the screen shot captures the page, or the reference material in general, and then you take a moment to highlight the important text that will later trigger your brain to help you remember your thought process.

5) Old school pen and pad (napkin/flyer/hand)

Obviously not an electronic means for capturing your best ideas, however, sometimes you just don’t have the right access to the tools that you need and you have to default to pen and paper.

Side note: I’m a huge fan of journaling everyday to keep your thoughts top of mind, and to help you work through your thoughts and concepts to stay creative and grateful in the world.

Quick story: I had been working on lyrics for a song for almost a year. I was struggling with how to tell the story of an incident that I had while on a college campus with a religious group. I wanted to tell the story in a song, however I didn’t want to specifically make it about that group. I just wanted to use my frustrations of that event to describe my mental frustrations and the plan for dealing with them.

I remember one morning hopping in the shower, humming the melody line of the song without lyric content (therefore making shapes and sounds in my head of my ideas for how the lyrics would be laid out). And out of nowhere, coherent literary gold started pouring out of my mind, just as I was physically applying shampoo into my hair as I washed it. I paused for a second.. thought about what I was saying, realized that I had a huge stroke of inspiration, and I had to decide quickly what I was going to do to capture it.

With soap still in my hair, basically in mid-shower, I stepped out, ran into the kitchen, grabbed a pencil and a nearby piece of paper, and wrote about 5 lines of text. The next week as I entered the studio, I reflected on how I came about that stream of consciousness, and just had to smile, “It’s always in the freaking shower.. the best ideas always come at the strangest times.”

But what you do with that piece of paper is extremely important!

Run it through Steps 1 through 4 above! Take a photo. Add the photo to a note. Send it into a note organizer like Evernote if you need to in order to add it to a collection of other like minded ideas. Edit the photo.. add highlights, circles, and more info to the photo to broaden the topic or help you expand on the idea for later. Send it to a Reminder or add it to the information in a task manager like OmniFocus!

Overall: Make sure that you have a workflow for allowing the concept to bubble back up to the surface of your digital life! You’ll want to see and work with the idea down the road, like when you are writing your next article, or social media share, or in my case, writing lyrics to a song!
